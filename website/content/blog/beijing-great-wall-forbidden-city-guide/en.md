Beijing is where most first trips to China either start or pass through, and for good reason. Within a few hours of the city stands the Great Wall; in its centre lies the Forbidden City, the palace of Ming and Qing emperors for nearly five centuries. Around them you find imperial temples and gardens, narrow hutong lanes and some of the best food in northern China.

The capital is also huge, and a little planning makes the difference between a rushed trip and a great one. This guide explains which Great Wall section to pick, how Forbidden City tickets work, how many days you need and when to go.

## The Great Wall: which section to choose

The Great Wall was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987. Near Beijing there are several sections open to visitors, and they feel very different. The three most commonly chosen are Mutianyu, Badaling and Jinshanling.

![The Great Wall near Beijing](photo:great-wall)

### Mutianyu

Roughly 70 km from central Beijing, usually 1.5–2 hours by car. Mutianyu is restored, surrounded by forested hills, and has a cable car, so you do not need to climb all the way up on foot. It is often the best balance for first-time visitors: impressive views, manageable effort and fewer crowds than Badaling.

### Badaling

The most famous and most visited section, a little closer to the city and reachable by rail as well as by road. It is fully restored and well equipped, which makes it convenient, but it can be very crowded, especially on weekends and Chinese public holidays.

### Jinshanling

Further away, around 125–130 km from Beijing, so expect 2.5–3 hours each way. Only partly restored, with watchtowers stretching along the ridges, it is loved by photographers and hikers. Choose it if you are fit, have a full day and want a quieter, wilder Wall.

**In short:** first visit and limited time — Mutianyu; easiest logistics — Badaling; scenery and hiking — Jinshanling.

**Tips for the Wall:** wear shoes with good grip (the steps are steep and uneven), bring water, and start early to beat the crowds. In winter, the stones can be icy — take it slowly.

## The Forbidden City (Palace Museum)

The former imperial palace, today the Palace Museum, was inscribed by UNESCO in 1987 as part of the Imperial Palaces of the Ming and Qing Dynasties. You enter from the south through the Meridian Gate and walk north through a succession of courtyards and halls; even a fast visit takes three hours.

> **Important:** tickets are not sold at the gate. Every visitor needs a real-name reservation made in advance through the museum's official booking channels, and foreigners book with their passport number. Bring the same original passport on the day — it is checked at entry. The museum is normally closed on Mondays (except some national holidays). Tickets are released a limited number of days ahead and popular dates sell out fast, so check the current booking rules and release time before your trip.

**Tip:** after the visit, climb the hill in Jingshan Park, just north of the palace exit, for the classic view over its golden roofs.

## More highlights of Beijing

### Temple of Heaven

A vast park where emperors performed rites for good harvests, inscribed by UNESCO in 1998. Its round Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests is one of the symbols of Beijing. Come in the morning to see locals doing tai chi, singing and playing cards in the park.

### Summer Palace

An imperial garden on the shore of Kunming Lake in the north-west of the city, also a UNESCO site since 1998. Walk the Long Corridor, climb Longevity Hill and in winter watch people skating on the frozen lake. Allow half a day.

### Hutongs

The old residential lanes around Shichahai (Houhai) and the Drum and Bell Towers show everyday Beijing. Explore on foot or by bicycle rickshaw, step into a courtyard café and look out for small family restaurants. Some lanes, like Nanluoguxiang, are touristy; the side lanes are calmer.

### Tiananmen Square

Directly south of the Forbidden City. Expect security checks and, at times, advance registration; check the current procedure before going.

## How many days do you need?

- **3 days** – Great Wall (one full day), Forbidden City and Jingshan Park, Temple of Heaven and the hutongs.
- **4 days** – add the Summer Palace and a slower pace.
- **5 days or more** – a second section of the Wall, museums, the Olympic Park or more neighbourhoods.

## When to go

- **Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October)** are the most comfortable, with mild temperatures and often clear skies. Autumn colours on the Wall are beautiful.
- **Summer** is hot and humid, and it is peak season for domestic travel.
- **Winter (December–February)** is cold and dry, often below zero, but skies are frequently clear and the crowds are much thinner. The Great Wall under snow is a rare and beautiful sight. Beijing pairs naturally with Harbin — see our [Harbin Ice Festival guide](/en/blog/harbin-ice-festival-guide/).
- Try to avoid the Golden Week holiday in early October and Chinese New Year, when attractions are at their busiest.

## Getting around Beijing

- **Metro:** an extensive network that reaches most sights; signs and announcements are in English. Expect security checks at station entrances.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing:** convenient with a Chinese ride-hailing app; have your destination written in Chinese.
- **Airports:** Beijing has two international airports, Capital (PEK) and Daxing (PKX), both linked to the city by express rail.
- **High-speed rail hubs:** trains leave from several stations, including Beijing South, Beijing West, Beijing Chaoyang (trains to Harbin and the northeast) and Beijing North. Always check which station your ticket is for — they are far apart.

## What to eat in Beijing

- **Peking duck** – crisp lacquered skin, carved at the table and wrapped in thin pancakes with scallions and sweet sauce.
- **Jiaozi** – dumplings with dozens of fillings, a northern staple.
- **Zhajiangmian** – thick wheat noodles with a savoury fermented soybean and pork sauce.
- Street snacks such as **jianbing** (savoury crepe) for breakfast.

## Practical tips

- Check current entry requirements for China for your nationality before booking.
- Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign card; many places prefer mobile payment.
- Carry your passport daily: it is needed for train tickets, hotels and many attraction reservations.
- Reserve popular sights early — not only the Forbidden City, many major sites now use online real-name booking.
- Some international websites and apps do not work in China without special arrangements; prepare offline maps and translation apps.

## FAQ

### Do I need to book the Forbidden City in advance?

Yes. Tickets are not sold on site; reserve online in advance with your passport details and bring the same passport.

### Which section of the Great Wall is best for a first visit?

For most people Mutianyu: restored, scenic, with a cable car and fewer crowds than Badaling.

### Is Beijing worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you dress warmly. Sights are calmer, the air is often clear, and you can combine the city with the ice festival in Harbin.

### How many days are enough for Beijing?

Three days cover the essentials; four give you a comfortable pace.
