Once you have decided to see China with a guide rather than entirely on your own, the next question is the format: join a group, or book a private tour just for you?

Both work well, and neither is "better" in general. They solve different problems. At Insta Tour we run both, so we have no reason to push you one way. Below is an honest comparison to help you decide.

## The short answer

- **Choose a small group tour** if you want a well-tested route at a fixed price, enjoy meeting people, and are happy to travel on set dates.
- **Choose a private tour** if you need your own dates, your own pace, a route built around specific interests, or you are travelling as a family or a group of friends who want to stay together.

Now the details.

## Cost: why groups are usually cheaper

Most costs on a tour are either **per person** (entry tickets, train seats, meals) or **shared** (the guide, the vehicle, the driver, the guide's own travel and hotel).

On a group tour, the shared costs are split between everyone. On a private tour, you carry them alone. That's the main reason a private trip for two usually costs more per person than the same route in a group, and why a private tour for six friends can come surprisingly close to group prices.

Other factors that change the price of a private tour:

- hotel category and room type
- season (national holidays push prices up across China)
- how many internal flights or long transfers the route needs
- the language of the guide and how many days you need them

That's why private routes are priced individually, not from a fixed list.

## Flexibility and pace

**Group tour.** The itinerary, hotels and daily rhythm are fixed in advance. You get a proven sequence: transfers, trains and visits are already timed. The trade-off is less room for spontaneous changes. If you want an extra afternoon in a tea house, it's harder to arrange.

**Private tour.** You choose when to start, how early to wake up, how long to stay at each place and what to skip. You can add a cooking class, a rest day or a detour for a photo spot. The trade-off: more decisions to make before the trip.

A useful rule: if you already know exactly what you want to see, go private. If you'd rather someone experienced planned it, a group works well.

## The social side

Travelling in a group means shared dinners, someone to laugh with when a menu translation goes wrong, and often friendships that last beyond the trip. For solo travellers this is a real advantage, as China can feel overwhelming alone.

A private tour gives you privacy and quiet. It suits honeymooners, families with their own routines and people who simply prefer not to share their holiday with strangers.

## What a small group of 5–7 people means in practice

"Group tour" often brings to mind a bus of 40 people following a flag. A small group is a very different experience:

- **One minivan, not a coach.** Getting in and out is quick, and you don't wait for dozens of people.
- **You can actually hear the guide** and ask questions without a microphone.
- **Restaurants are easier.** Seven people fit at one round table, which is the best way to share Chinese dishes.
- **The pace adapts.** With a few people, the guide can notice if someone is tired or wants more time.
- **Less queuing logistics** at stations, viewpoints and ticket gates.

That's why Insta Tour keeps its groups to 5–7 travellers.

## Who each format suits

### First-time visitors

Both work. A small group is often ideal: the route is proven, everything is organised and you have company. If your dates are fixed by work or school holidays, a private tour removes the dependence on group departure dates.

### Couples

Couples who like meeting people do well in small groups. For a honeymoon or anniversary, a private route with a slower pace and special hotels is often worth the difference.

### Families with children

Private is usually more comfortable: you control nap times, meal times and the number of museums in a day. Children rarely enjoy someone else's schedule.

### Groups of friends

If you are four to seven people already, a private tour gives you your own vehicle and guide, and the price per person may be close to a group tour.

### Photographers

Private wins. Sunrise and sunset light don't fit into a typical group timetable, and you may want to spend an hour at one viewpoint.

## What's usually included and what's extra

Every operator is different, so always read the inclusions list. Typically **included** in Chinese tours:

- accommodation and some meals
- transport between cities (trains, sometimes domestic flights) and transfers
- the guide's services
- entrance tickets for the sights in the programme

Typically **extra**:

- international flights to and from China
- visa costs, if your citizenship requires a visa
- travel insurance
- some meals, drinks and personal expenses
- optional activities and tips

## Questions to ask any tour operator

Whichever company you choose, these questions save you trouble:

1. **What is the maximum group size?** "Small group" can mean 7 or 25.
2. **What exactly is included?** Ask about meals, entry tickets, train and flight tickets, transfers.
3. **In which language does the guide speak?** And are they with you the whole trip or only in some cities?
4. **What happens if the minimum number isn't reached?** Will the tour be cancelled, moved or run anyway?
5. **What is the cancellation and refund policy?**
6. **How intense is the programme?** How many hotel changes, early starts and long walking days are there?
7. **Which hotels are used?** Ask for names or at least the category and location.
8. **Do they help with entry requirements?** A good operator explains the current rules but never guarantees a visa or entry.

## FAQ

### Is a group tour in China cheaper than a private tour?

Usually, per person, yes, because the guide, vehicle and driver costs are shared. For a group of friends or a larger family, a private tour can come close to group pricing.

### Is a small group tour good for solo travellers?

Very much so. You get company, a set route and organised logistics, which is especially helpful in China, where the language and apps can be challenging alone.

### Can I customise a group tour?

Group itineraries are fixed so everyone gets what they booked. If you want changes, a private tour is the right format. Contact us and we'll discuss a custom route.

### How far in advance should I book?

As early as you can, especially for trips during Chinese public holidays, when trains and hotels fill quickly. Check entry rules for your citizenship at the same time.
