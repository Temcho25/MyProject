Thousands of stone pillars rise out of the forest, their tops covered in pines and often wrapped in cloud. If you have seen the film *Avatar*, the scene will look strangely familiar. This is Zhangjiajie, in the north-west of China's Hunan province, and for many travellers it is the moment China stops being "cities and temples" and becomes one of the most dramatic natural landscapes on the planet.

This guide explains what is actually where, how to split your days, when to go and how to get there, so your first visit goes smoothly.

## Wulingyuan: the area behind the "Avatar mountains"

The pillars most people picture are inside the **Wulingyuan Scenic and Historic Interest Area**, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1992. It contains more than 3,000 quartzite sandstone pillars and peaks, many over 200 metres tall, shaped over millions of years by water and erosion.

Wulingyuan brings together several protected areas. The ones you will hear about most are:

- **Zhangjiajie National Forest Park** — founded in 1982 as China's first national forest park. Golden Whip Stream and Yuanjiajie are reached from here.
- **Tianzi Mountain** — a high plateau with wide panoramic viewpoints over the "forest" of peaks.
- **Suoxi Valley** and **Yangjiajie** — quieter sections with gorges, caves and ridge walks.

One thing that surprises first-timers: Wulingyuan is not a single viewpoint. It is a large mountain area with eco-buses, cable cars, stairs and an elevator connecting the scenic zones. Planning a route matters more than in most parks.

### Yuanjiajie and the "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain"

Yuanjiajie is the part most associated with the film. Here you'll find the **Qiankun Column** (also known as the Southern Sky Column), a lone pillar topped with trees. In January 2010, after *Avatar* became a global hit, local authorities officially renamed it "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain". The city's claim was that photographs taken in Zhangjiajie in 2008 helped inspire Pandora's floating mountains.

To be accurate: this link is widely reported and enthusiastically promoted locally, but the film's floating mountains are a design created by the filmmakers, and other Chinese landscapes (such as Huangshan) have also been named as influences. So it's fair to say Zhangjiajie is *widely regarded as an inspiration* for the Avatar mountains — and once you stand on the viewing platforms, it's easy to see why.

### Golden Whip Stream

A gentle trail follows the **Golden Whip Stream** along the valley floor, between towering cliffs and clear water. It is mostly flat, shaded and great in summer heat. You see the pillars from below, which is a completely different experience to the platforms on top. Watch your snacks — the local macaques are bold.

### Bailong Elevator

The **Bailong Elevator** carries visitors up a cliff face towards Yuanjiajie. It is 326 metres high and recognised by Guinness World Records as the world's tallest outdoor lift. The ride itself lasts only a minute or two, but at busy times the queue can be long, so build it into your timing. It usually requires its own ticket in addition to park entry.

### Tianzi Mountain

**Tianzi Mountain** offers some of the widest views over the pillar landscape. It is connected to the rest of the park by eco-bus and also has its own cable car. On a clear morning, or when cloud sits between the peaks, this is where many of the classic panoramic photographs are taken.

## Tianmen Mountain: Heaven's Gate and the glass skywalk

**Tianmen Mountain** is a separate attraction close to Zhangjiajie city, with its own ticket. Its highlights:

- **The cable car** — starts in the city and climbs about 7.5 km to the mountain, one of the longest passenger cableways of its kind. The ride alone is a reason to come.
- **Heaven's Gate (Tianmen Cave)** — a natural arch about 130 m high in the mountain's rock wall, reached by a famous staircase of 999 steps (escalators inside the mountain are an alternative for those who prefer them).
- **Tongtian Avenue** — a winding road with 99 bends, which you can see from above.
- **Glass skywalks** — cliff paths with glass floor sections, including the Coiling Dragon Cliff skywalk.

![Tianmen Mountain in Zhangjiajie](photo:tianmen-mountain)

Tianmen deserves a full day of its own. Combining it with Wulingyuan on the same day is not realistic.

## The Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon glass bridge

The **glass bridge over the Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon** opened in 2016. It is about 430 m long and hangs roughly 300 m above the canyon floor. It is outside Wulingyuan, so it needs extra time and a separate ticket.

> **Important:** The bridge has been temporarily closed in the past (including shortly after opening, due to crowds), and access rules can change. Check that it is open and how tickets are sold shortly before your trip.

## How many days do you need in Zhangjiajie?

- **2 days** — the minimum: one day for Wulingyuan (Yuanjiajie, Tianzi Mountain), one for Tianmen Mountain.
- **3 days** — the comfortable option: two days in Wulingyuan (add Golden Whip Stream and quieter viewpoints) plus Tianmen.
- **4 days** — if you want the glass bridge too, or simply like walking at a slow pace and waiting for the fog to clear.

Wulingyuan is a long way from Tianmen, so sleeping near the park entrance in Wulingyuan for the park days saves a lot of transport time.

## Best time to visit and the famous fog

Zhangjiajie has a humid subtropical climate, and the weather shapes the experience:

- **Spring (April–May)** — green, mild, often misty. Expect rain.
- **Summer (June–August)** — hot, humid and busy; heavy rain and sea-of-cloud scenes are common.
- **Autumn (September–November)** — many people's favourite: clearer air, comfortable walking temperatures, autumn colours later in the season.
- **Winter** — cold and quiet, sometimes with snow or ice on the peaks; some routes may close in bad weather.

Fog is part of the magic: pillars appearing and disappearing in the clouds are exactly what makes the "Avatar" look. But on some days visibility drops to almost nothing. That's another reason to allow an extra day.

Avoid Chinese public holidays if you can, especially the first week of October and the May Day holiday. Crowds then can be overwhelming.

## How to get to Zhangjiajie

- **By high-speed train** — the main gateway for many travellers is **Zhangjiajie West** high-speed railway station. From **Changsha**, the provincial capital, the train journey takes roughly two and a half to three and a half hours, depending on the service.
- **By plane** — Zhangjiajie has its own airport near the city, with domestic flights from major Chinese cities.
- **From the city to Wulingyuan** — Wulingyuan is roughly 40 minutes to an hour from the city by road; buses and taxis run there.

International travellers usually fly into a large hub first, such as Changsha or another major city, and continue by train or domestic flight. Check current routes and schedules when you plan.

## Practical tips

- **Tickets:** The Wulingyuan ticket is a multi-day ticket — usually valid for 4 consecutive days — and includes the park's eco-buses. Cable cars and the Bailong Elevator are typically extra and one-way. Rules on reservations, start dates and passport registration change, so confirm the details when you book.
- **Passport:** Bring it every day. It is used for ticket checks.
- **Shoes:** Comfortable, non-slip walking shoes are a must. There are many stairs and steps get wet in mist.
- **Rain gear:** A light raincoat is more practical than an umbrella on narrow paths.
- **Crowds:** Start early. The first hours after opening are calmer, and the light is often softer.
- **Cash and payments:** Mobile payments dominate in China; set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you go, and carry some cash just in case.

## FAQ

### Are the Avatar mountains real?

The film's floating mountains are fictional, but the pillar landscape widely seen as their inspiration is real. It is in Wulingyuan, Zhangjiajie, Hunan province. The Qiankun Column at Yuanjiajie was officially renamed "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain" in 2010.

### Is Tianmen Mountain part of Zhangjiajie National Forest Park?

No. Tianmen Mountain is a separate scenic area near Zhangjiajie city with its own ticket. Plan a separate day for it.

### Is Zhangjiajie hard to walk?

You can see a lot using eco-buses, cable cars and the elevator, but expect plenty of stairs. A moderate level of fitness and good shoes make the trip far more enjoyable.

### When is the fog most likely?

Mist and low cloud are common in spring and summer and after rain at any time of year. Fog can't be guaranteed, so if it matters to you, allow an extra day.
