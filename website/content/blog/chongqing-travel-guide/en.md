Chongqing does not look like any other city. It sits on steep hills where the Yangtze and Jialing rivers meet, so streets are stacked on top of each other, bridges link one cliff to another, and a building's "ground floor" can be many storeys above the street on the other side. Chinese social media nicknamed it the "8D city" because maps barely help here – you navigate in three dimensions. This guide covers what to see, how to plan your days, what to eat and how to cope with the stairs, the heat and the spice.

## Why Chongqing is called the "8D city"

The old centre, Yuzhong, is a narrow peninsula between two rivers. With so little flat land, the city grew upwards and downwards at the same time. You can walk out of a mall on one level and find yourself on a busy road; take a lift and arrive at a park; follow a staircase and end up by the river. Navigation apps sometimes struggle with the levels, so allow extra time and enjoy getting a little lost – it is part of the experience.

## Top things to see in Chongqing

### Liziba: the train that goes through a building

Liziba station on monorail Line 2 sits inside a residential building – trains enter the block, stop at the station on the middle floors and continue out the other side. The station and building were designed and built together, so it is not an accident of planning, but it still looks surreal. There is a viewing platform at street level below the station where you can wait for a train to "disappear" into the building. Better still, ride Line 2 itself: part of the route runs along the Jialing River with great views.

### Hongyadong at night

Hongyadong is a complex of stilted wooden-style buildings stacked along a cliff above the Jialing River, close to where it meets the Yangtze. Inside are shops, restaurants and snack stalls on many levels. It is pleasant by day, but the real reason to come is the evening, when the façade lights up in gold and its reflections shimmer on the water. Many visitors compare it to a scene from an animated film.

For the classic photo, cross to the opposite bank or the nearby bridge rather than standing inside the complex. Evenings, especially weekends and holidays, are very crowded – keep an eye on your belongings and expect slow-moving queues on the walkways.

### Jiefangbei and the city centre

Jiefangbei, the Liberation Monument, stands in the heart of Yuzhong, surrounded by shopping streets and skyscrapers. It is a good starting point: Hongyadong, the cableway and several food streets are within walking distance – though "walking distance" in Chongqing often includes stairs.

### Yangtze River Cableway

This aerial tramway has crossed the Yangtze since 1987, linking Yuzhong with the Nan'an district on the south bank. Once a commuter route, it is now mainly a sightseeing ride of a few minutes over the river and between towers. Queues can be very long on weekends and in the evening. Travellers often recommend going early in the morning or late in the evening, and riding one way rather than returning in the same queue. Ticket prices and opening hours change – check them on the day.

### Ciqikou Ancient Town

Ciqikou, in the Shapingba district on the Jialing River, is a historic riverside town of stone lanes and old wooden houses. Today it is mostly a lively market street with tea houses, local snacks and handicrafts. It gets very busy on weekends; arrive in the morning on a weekday and wander into the quieter side lanes.

### Two rivers and the night views

The Yangtze and the Jialing rivers meet at Chaotianmen, at the tip of the Yuzhong peninsula. You can sometimes see the difference in water colour where they join. After dark, Chongqing becomes one of China's great night cities: lit-up bridges, skyscrapers on both banks and boats on the water. Options include an evening river cruise, viewpoints on the hills of the south bank, or simply a walk along the riverside.

## Hot pot: Chongqing's signature dish

Chongqing claims to be the home of Sichuan-style hot pot, and it takes the dish seriously. The broth is rich with beef tallow, dried chillies and Sichuan pepper, creating the numbing, fiery "mala" sensation. Diners cook beef tripe, duck intestines, meat slices, tofu and vegetables in the pot, then dip them in a sesame oil and garlic sauce that softens the heat.

A few tips for first-timers:

- Order a split "yuanyang" pot with a mild broth on one side.
- Ask for "wei la" (mildly spicy) – "medium" in Chongqing is very hot for most visitors.
- Have a cold drink or soy milk on the table; it helps more than water.
- Try "xiao mian", Chongqing's spicy noodle breakfast, and suan la fen (hot and sour sweet-potato noodles) from street stalls.

## How many days do you need?

- **1 day:** Liziba, Jiefangbei, the cableway and Hongyadong at night. Possible, but tiring.
- **2 days:** the ideal minimum – add Ciqikou, a river view in the evening and a proper hot pot dinner.
- **3 days:** time for a river cruise, quieter neighbourhoods and the south-bank viewpoints without rushing.

Many travellers combine Chongqing with Chengdu, which is close by high-speed train. Read our [Chengdu and pandas guide](/en/blog/chengdu-pandas-guide/) to plan both.

## Best time to visit Chongqing

Spring (roughly March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the most comfortable seasons. Summers are hot and humid – Chongqing is traditionally known as one of China's "furnace cities" – so plan sightseeing for mornings and evenings if you visit in July or August. The city is also famous for fog, especially in the cooler months, which can soften views from viewpoints but gives the skyline a moody, cinematic look. Winter is grey and damp but rarely freezing.

## Getting there

- **By train from Chengdu:** the fastest high-speed trains take a little over an hour, most take about 1–2 hours depending on the stations and stops. Check current timetables before travel.
- **By air:** Chongqing Jiangbei International Airport has domestic connections across China and some international routes. Travellers from Georgia and Europe usually connect via a Chinese or Middle Eastern hub – check current routes when booking.
- **Within the city:** the metro and monorail network is extensive and cheap. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are useful, but traffic and one-way hill roads can make short distances slow.

## Practical tips

- **Wear comfortable shoes.** Chongqing has endless staircases, slopes and steep alleys. Leave the new sandals at home.
- **Pace yourself with spice.** Even "mild" can be quite spicy. Keep some bland snacks for your stomach.
- **Allow extra time.** Distances on the map are not the real distances – stairs, lifts and bridges add time.
- **Carry your passport.** You need it for trains, hotels and some attractions.
- **Set up mobile payment** (Alipay or WeChat Pay) before the trip.
- **Book hotels near a metro station** in Yuzhong or Jiangbei to save energy.

## FAQ

### Is Chongqing worth visiting?

Yes – especially if you enjoy cities, architecture and food. Its vertical layout, river views and night lights are unlike anywhere else in China.

### Where is the best place to see Hongyadong at night?

From across the river or from the nearby bridge, where you can see the whole lit-up façade and its reflection. Inside the complex it is crowded and you only see fragments.

### Is it easy to travel from Chengdu to Chongqing?

Very easy. High-speed trains run frequently, and the fastest take roughly an hour. Check the current timetable and book with your passport.

### Is Chongqing too hot in summer?

It can be very hot and humid in July and August. If you travel then, sightsee early and late, rest at midday and drink plenty of water.
