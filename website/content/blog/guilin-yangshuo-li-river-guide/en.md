Green limestone peaks stand along a slow river, water buffalo wade in the shallows and bamboo rafts drift past rice fields. For centuries Chinese painters and poets have returned to this landscape, and it is still one of the most beautiful places you can visit in China. Guilin and Yangshuo, in the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region in the south of the country, are the classic base for exploring it.

This guide covers what to see, how to split your time between Guilin and Yangshuo, when to go and how to get around, especially if it's your first trip to China.

## Why the karst landscape is special

The rounded, steep hills around Guilin are karst towers: limestone shaped over a very long time by rain and rivers into isolated peaks, caves and underground streams. Guilin's karst is part of the **South China Karst** UNESCO World Heritage Site; it was added in the 2014 extension of the listing.

What makes the area so easy to enjoy is that the peaks are not remote. They rise right next to villages, fields, roads and the river, so you can experience them by boat, by bike or on foot.

## The Li River cruise: Guilin to Yangshuo

The **Li River (Lijiang) cruise** is the signature experience of the region. The classic route starts from a pier south of Guilin city and ends in Yangshuo, a journey of roughly four hours along one of the most scenic stretches of the river. Along the way the boat passes bamboo groves, cliff faces with names like "Nine Horses Fresco Hill", fishing villages and endless rows of peaks.

A few things worth knowing:

- **Boat types** — there are larger cruise boats with lunch on board and smaller bamboo-style rafts that cover shorter sections. The experience and price differ, so choose based on how much time you have.
- **Water levels** — in the drier months the river can be low, and the route or departure pier may change. Check with the operator before you go.
- **Return** — most travellers continue to Yangshuo and stay there, rather than going back to Guilin the same day.

### The 20-yuan note view at Xingping

Near the old town of **Xingping**, the river bends past a group of peaks reflected in the water at **Yellow Cloth Shoal**. This is the scene on the back of the current 20-yuan banknote. Take a note with you; holding it up against the real view is the photo almost everyone takes. Xingping itself is a quiet old riverside town and worth a walk if you have time.

## Yangshuo: countryside, rafting and cycling

**Yangshuo** is a small town surrounded by countryside, and it is often the highlight of the trip. Many travellers spend more time here than in Guilin.

### Yulong River bamboo rafting

The **Yulong River** is a smaller, calmer tributary of the Li. A ride on a bamboo raft, poled by a local boatman, takes you past fields, peaks and old stone bridges, including the centuries-old **Yulong (Dragon) Bridge**. It is slow, quiet and one of the most relaxing things you can do here.

![Bamboo rafts on the Yulong River in Yangshuo](photo:yangshuo-yulong-river)

### Cycling between the peaks

Yangshuo is ideal for cycling. Country lanes run between rice fields and villages along the Yulong River, mostly flat. You can rent a bike or e-bike in town. Expect some shared traffic on narrow roads and ride carefully.

### Sunrise and sunset viewpoints

For a view over the river and peaks from above, many people climb **Xianggong Hill** near Xingping at sunrise. The climb is short but steep. Within Yangshuo, there are several small hills with lookouts over the town.

### West Street

**West Street** is Yangshuo's lively pedestrian street, full of cafés, restaurants, bars and souvenir shops. It is busy and touristy, especially in the evening, but it's a fun place to try local food and watch the town come alive. If you prefer peace and quiet, stay in a guesthouse in the countryside outside town instead.

## Guilin city sights

Guilin itself is a pleasant green city with rivers and lakes running through it. Two classic sights are easy to fit into half a day:

- **Elephant Trunk Hill** — the city's symbol: a karst hill on the riverbank with a natural arch that looks like an elephant drinking from the river.
- **Reed Flute Cave** — a karst cave with stalactites and stone formations, lit with coloured lights. A good choice on a rainy day.

The lakes and evening river walks in the city centre are also pleasant if you have a spare evening.

## How many days do you need?

- **2 days** — the minimum: Li River cruise to Yangshuo, then a day of rafting and cycling.
- **3–4 days** — the ideal: add Guilin's city sights, Xingping and a sunrise viewpoint, without rushing.
- **5 days or more** — add the Longji rice terraces north of Guilin, or simply slow down in the countryside.

## Best time to visit

The climate is subtropical, with hot, humid summers and mild winters.

- **Spring (April–May)** — green landscapes and frequent mist on the peaks. This is the start of the rainy season.
- **Summer (June–August)** — hot and rainy, but the river is full and the landscape at its most lush. Heavy rain can occasionally affect river trips.
- **Autumn (September–November)** — often the most comfortable season: drier, clearer and pleasant for cycling.
- **Winter (December–February)** — cool and quiet. The river is lower, but misty mornings can be beautiful.

Many photographers actually love the rainy season: low clouds between the peaks create exactly the "Chinese painting" look. Pack a raincoat and enjoy it. As everywhere in China, Chinese national holidays (early October, the May Day holiday) bring big crowds.

## How to get there

- **By plane** — Guilin has an international airport with flights from many Chinese cities.
- **By high-speed train** — Guilin is well connected by high-speed rail. The city has several stations, so check which one your ticket uses.
- **To Yangshuo** — the most scenic way is the Li River cruise. By road it's around an hour or more from Guilin. **Yangshuo railway station** on the high-speed line is near Xingping, not in Yangshuo town itself, so allow extra time for a transfer.

From Georgia or Russia, travellers usually reach Guilin via a major Chinese hub city and then continue by domestic flight or high-speed train. Check current routes when planning.

## What to eat

- **Guilin rice noodles (Guilin mifen)** — the local breakfast and favourite at any time of day: soft rice noodles with broth, pickles, peanuts and meat. Each shop has its own recipe.
- **Beer fish** — Yangshuo's signature dish: river fish cooked with beer, tomatoes, peppers and spices. You'll find it everywhere around West Street.
- **Stuffed dishes (niang)** — vegetables such as peppers, tofu or snails stuffed with minced meat, a local specialty.

## Practical tips

- **Shoes:** Comfortable shoes with good grip are useful — paths and rafts can be wet.
- **Rain:** Bring a light raincoat in spring and summer.
- **Sun:** On the river and while cycling there is little shade; use a hat and sunscreen.
- **Bookings:** Li River cruises and Yulong rafting are popular; in high season, book ahead. Operators and routes change, so confirm details when booking.
- **Payments:** Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before the trip and carry some cash.

## FAQ

### Should I stay in Guilin or Yangshuo?

For most travellers, Yangshuo is the better base: the countryside, rafting and cycling are right outside the door. Spend a night or half a day in Guilin for the city sights.

### Where is the 20-yuan note view?

At Yellow Cloth Shoal on the Li River near Xingping old town, in Yangshuo County. You can see it from boats on the river and from the riverbank.

### Is the rainy season a bad time to go?

Not necessarily. Rain and mist often make the karst landscape more atmospheric. Just allow flexibility, as heavy rain can sometimes disrupt river activities.

### How long is the Li River cruise?

The classic cruise from near Guilin to Yangshuo takes about four hours. Shorter raft trips around Xingping are also available.
