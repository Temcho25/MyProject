For many travellers, the first ride on a Chinese bullet train is one of the most memorable moments of the trip. Stations look like airports, carriages are quiet and clean, and the countryside slides past at a speed you only notice when you see the cars on the motorway falling behind. The good news: once you understand a few rules, the system is very easy for foreigners to use. This guide explains train types, seat classes, how to buy tickets with a passport and what to expect at the station.

## What China's high-speed trains are

China has the world's largest high-speed rail network, and for travel between major tourist cities it is usually the most convenient option. You can tell the type of train from the letter at the start of the train number:

- **G trains** — the fastest category, running on dedicated high-speed lines. On the best lines they run at up to 350 km/h.
- **D trains** — "bullet trains" that are also fast but usually slower than G trains; some run on lines shared with conventional trains. There are also overnight D trains with sleeper berths on certain routes.
- **C trains** — intercity trains between nearby cities, for example within one province or metropolitan region.

You will often hear the name **Fuxing** (复兴号) — the modern generation of Chinese-built high-speed trains. Older models are called **Hexie** (和谐号). For a passenger, the difference is mostly in comfort and onboard features; both are clean and reliable.

## Why the train is often better than flying

- **City centre to city centre.** Many high-speed stations are connected to the metro, while airports are often far out of town.
- **Less waiting.** Security at railway stations is quicker than at airports, and you do not need to arrive hours early.
- **Fewer delays.** Trains are less affected by weather than flights, which matters if your itinerary is tight.
- **Comfort.** Legroom, freedom to walk around, power sockets at most seats, and views from the window.

For journeys of up to five or six hours, the train usually wins. For very long distances — for example from the far north to the south — a domestic flight may make more sense.

## Seat classes

Most high-speed trains offer three classes:

- **Second class** — the standard choice, with 3+2 seating. Comfortable enough for several hours, and what most Chinese travellers use.
- **First class** — 2+2 seating, wider seats and more legroom. A good upgrade for longer journeys or if you value a quieter carriage.
- **Business class** — the premium option on many G trains, with large reclining seats and a small number of places. Availability depends on the train.

Ticket prices depend on the route, the train and the class, so check the current fare when booking rather than relying on figures from old blogs.

## How to buy tickets as a foreigner

China uses **real-name ticketing**: every ticket is linked to a passenger's identity document. For tourists this means your passport. There are three main ways to buy.

### 1. The official 12306 website or app

China Railway's official platform, 12306, has an English version of its website and app, and foreign passport holders can register. The process in general terms:

1. Create an account and choose "passport" as your document type.
2. Complete identity verification. Your passport details are checked; if automatic verification does not work, you can usually upload a photo of your passport page for manual review, or verify at a station counter.
3. Search for your route, choose a train and seat, add yourself as a passenger and pay. Foreign bank cards and mobile payment apps are listed as payment options, but in practice not every card works — have a backup.

Booking on 12306 means no agency fees, but the interface and verification can take some patience.

### 2. At the station

You can buy at ticket counters in railway stations by showing your passport. Counters accept cash in yuan, bank cards and mobile payments. Queues can be long at big stations, especially before holidays, and popular trains may already be sold out on the day — so this works better as a backup than as a plan.

### 3. Via travel platforms

International online travel agencies sell Chinese train tickets in English and accept foreign cards. They usually charge a service fee, but many first-time travellers find them easier. The ticket is still issued in your name and linked to your passport.

> **Important:** Rules for registration, verification and payment change from time to time. Check the current requirements on the official 12306 English site before your trip, and always book with exactly the same passport you will carry — you will need it to enter the station and the platform.

### Changes and refunds

Tickets can usually be changed or refunded on 12306 or at a station counter. Service fees may apply depending on how close to departure you make the change.

## At the station: what to expect

### Security and passport checks

On arrival you pass an ID check and a security screening similar to an airport: bags go through an X-ray machine. Liquids are generally allowed in reasonable amounts, but sharp objects, flammable items and other prohibited goods are not — check the current list if you are unsure.

Your passport is your ticket. Chinese ID cards open the automatic gates; foreign passports sometimes do too, but often you will use the staffed lane next to them. That is completely normal.

### Arrive early

At large stations allow **45–60 minutes** before departure: there may be a queue at security, the halls are huge, and the boarding gate for your train is announced on screens. Boarding usually starts some 15–20 minutes before departure, and the gates close a few minutes before the train leaves. Trains depart exactly on time and stops at intermediate stations are short — be ready on the platform.

### Finding your seat

Your booking shows the carriage number and seat. Floor markings on the platform show where each carriage stops. Seat letters follow a pattern: in second class, A and F are window seats; in first class, A and F are usually window seats as well.

## Luggage

Carry-on luggage limits exist, but a normal suitcase and a backpack per person are fine. Small bags go on the overhead rack; large suitcases go in the luggage area at the end of the carriage or behind the last row of seats. Travelling light makes everything easier — stairs and escalators at stations, and space on board. Check the current size and weight limits before the trip if you carry something unusual.

## Food on board

- **Hot water** dispensers are available on high-speed trains — many travellers bring instant noodles or tea.
- A **trolley** passes with snacks, drinks and simple meals, and many longer-distance trains have a dining car.
- Station halls have plenty of shops and cafés, so you can buy something before boarding.

The carriages are quiet: people speak softly and use headphones. Following this etiquette is appreciated.

## How trains fit into a tour route

On a typical China itinerary, the train connects the highlights smoothly:

- **Changsha – Zhangjiajie.** High-speed trains run to Zhangjiajie West station; depending on the train, the trip takes around 2–3.5 hours. This is how many travellers reach the "Avatar" mountains.
- **Xi'an – Luoyang.** The fastest G trains between Xi'an North and Luoyang Longmen take around 1.5 hours, which makes it easy to combine the Terracotta Army with the Longmen Grottoes and the Shaolin Temple area.
- **Beijing – Harbin.** The north-east is now linked to the capital by high-speed rail; the fastest trains take around 5–6 hours, a comfortable way to reach the ice city in winter.

Timetables change, so treat these as approximate and check the current schedule.

## FAQ

### Can foreigners buy Chinese train tickets online?

Yes. The official 12306 platform has an English website and app that accept foreign passports, and international travel platforms also sell tickets. Check the current registration and payment requirements before your trip.

### Do I need a paper ticket?

In most cases, no — your passport serves as your ticket at the gates. If you need a receipt for expenses, ask about it at the station or in the app.

### Which class should I choose?

Second class is perfectly comfortable for most journeys. Choose first class for longer trips or if you want more space; business class is a treat rather than a necessity.

### What if I miss my train?

Contact station staff or check the 12306 app straight away — depending on the rules in force, you may be able to rebook onto a later train. Arriving early is the best protection.
