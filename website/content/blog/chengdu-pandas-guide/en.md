Chengdu is the city most travellers picture when they think of giant pandas – and for good reason. The capital of Sichuan province is home to the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, but it is also one of the most relaxed big cities in China: people linger for hours over tea, the food is famously bold, and the pace feels slower than in Beijing or Shanghai. This guide covers how to plan the panda visit, what else is worth your time, what to eat, and how many days to allow.

## The Chengdu Panda Base: how to plan your visit

The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding is a large, green, park-like site in the north of the city. Pandas live in spacious enclosures among bamboo groves, and you walk between them on shaded paths. Allow at least half a day – the grounds are big, and the best moments come when you slow down and wait.

### Go early in the morning

This is the single most useful tip. Pandas tend to be most active in the cool morning hours, when they are fed and busy eating bamboo. By midday, especially in warm weather, many of them are asleep in a tree or tucked away in a corner of the enclosure. Aim to be at the gate around opening time.

According to the base's official website, morning entry starts at 7:30 from March to October and at 8:00 from November to February. Hours can change, so check the official site before your trip.

### Booking tickets in advance

Ticketing works through a real-name system. The official channels (the base's WeChat account and mini program) allow reservations up to 14 days ahead, and on-site sales also exist. In practice, though, popular days – weekends, Chinese public holidays and the summer season – can sell out, and foreign visitors are usually asked for their passport details when booking and to show the passport at the gate.

> **Important:** Booking rules, ticket prices and entry gates for foreign passport holders change from time to time. Check the current procedure on the official Panda Base website a few days before your visit, book ahead if you can, and carry your physical passport.

### Tips for the panda base

- Head first to the areas with adult pandas and the nursery, then explore at a slower pace.
- Use the sightseeing shuttle if you have limited time; the site is hilly in places.
- Be patient at each enclosure – a sleeping panda can wake up and start eating within minutes.
- Respect the rules: no flash, no tapping on glass, no feeding.
- Bring water and comfortable shoes. Summer mornings are humid.

## What else to see in Chengdu

Pandas are the headline, but Chengdu rewards a slower look at everyday life.

### Kuanzhai Alley (Wide and Narrow Alleys)

Three parallel lanes of restored courtyard houses in the city centre, now full of tea houses, snack stalls and craft shops. It is touristy, but the grey-brick architecture is attractive and it is a good place for a first taste of Sichuan street food. Early morning or a weekday evening is calmer.

### Jinli Ancient Street

A lively pedestrian street beside the Wuhou Shrine, dedicated to the Three Kingdoms era. Jinli is at its best after dark, when red lanterns light up the lanes and the snack stalls are busiest. Combine it with a visit to the shrine during the day.

### Wenshu Monastery

An active Buddhist monastery and one of the calmest places in the city centre. Walk through the courtyards, watch locals light incense, and stop at the vegetarian restaurant or tea garden. Dress modestly and keep your voice down in the halls.

### People's Park and its tea houses

If you want to understand Chengdu, spend an hour in People's Park. Locals play cards and mahjong, sing, dance and drink jasmine tea from lidded cups at outdoor tea houses. You will also see the ear-cleaning masters walking between tables with their tools – a famous local tradition, try it only if you feel comfortable.

### Sichuan opera and face-changing

Sichuan opera is known for "bian lian", the face-changing act in which performers switch masks in a fraction of a second. Evening shows in tourist theatres usually combine face-changing with fire-spitting, puppetry and short comic scenes, so you do not need to understand Chinese to enjoy it.

## Day trips from Chengdu (optional)

If you have an extra day, two classic excursions are possible – both are optional and depend on your pace:

- **Leshan Giant Buddha** – a huge Buddha carved into a riverside cliff. Leshan is reachable by high-speed train from Chengdu in roughly an hour; check current timetables, as journey times vary between trains.
- **Mount Emei** – one of China's sacred Buddhist mountains, with temples, forest trails and views from the summit area. It makes for a long day; many travellers prefer to stay overnight.

## Sichuan cuisine: what to eat

Sichuan food is the reason many people come back to Chengdu. Its signature is "mala" – a combination of chilli heat ("la") and the tingling, numbing effect of Sichuan pepper ("ma"). It is not just about being spicy: good Sichuan cooking balances heat with sour, sweet and savoury notes.

Dishes to try:

- **Mapo tofu** – soft tofu in a red, oily sauce with minced meat and Sichuan pepper. Simple and addictive.
- **Hot pot** – a bubbling pot of broth in the middle of the table where you cook meat, vegetables and tofu yourself. Order a split pot ("yuanyang") with a mild broth on one side if you are unsure.
- **Dandan noodles** – noodles with a spicy, nutty sauce and minced pork, usually a small bowl, perfect as a snack.
- **Kung pao chicken** – the Sichuan original is less sweet and more fragrant than versions abroad.
- **Street snacks** – skewers, spicy rabbit, sweet rice cakes and cold noodles in Kuanzhai Alley and Jinli.

If you do not handle spice well, learn to say "wei la" (mildly spicy) or "bu la" (not spicy). Restaurants are usually happy to adjust.

## How many days do you need in Chengdu?

- **1 day:** panda base in the morning, People's Park and Kuanzhai Alley in the afternoon, hot pot in the evening. Doable, but rushed.
- **2 days:** the comfortable minimum – pandas on day one, monasteries, tea houses and Sichuan opera on day two.
- **3 days:** add a day trip to Leshan or simply slow down and eat your way around the city.

## Best time to visit

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are the most pleasant seasons, with mild temperatures. Summer is hot and humid, and pandas are less active outside the early morning. Winter is cool, grey and damp, but crowds are thinner. Avoid Chinese national holidays (especially the first week of October) if you can – the panda base gets very busy.

## Getting to Chengdu

Chengdu has two international airports, Tianfu and Shuangliu, so check which one your ticket uses. The city is also a major high-speed rail hub: Chongqing is roughly 1–1.5 hours away by fast train, which makes the two cities an easy pair. Inside Chengdu, the metro is the simplest way to get around, and ride-hailing apps work well for short trips.

## Practical tips

- Carry your passport – you need it for train tickets, hotels and many attractions.
- Set up mobile payments (Alipay or WeChat Pay) before you travel; some smaller places do not accept foreign cards.
- Keep tissues and wet wipes with you – Sichuan food is messy and not every toilet has paper.
- Download an offline translation app; English is limited outside hotels.

## FAQ

### What time is best to see pandas in Chengdu?

Early morning, right after opening. Pandas are usually most active and eating during the cool morning hours; by midday many are sleeping.

### Do I need to book Panda Base tickets in advance?

It is strongly recommended, especially for weekends, holidays and summer. The base uses real-name booking, and foreigners usually need their passport details. Check the current rules on the official website before you go.

### Is Sichuan food too spicy for beginners?

Not necessarily. Many dishes are mild, and you can ask for "wei la" (a little spicy). For hot pot, a split pot with a non-spicy broth is a safe start.

### Is Chengdu worth it without the pandas?

Yes. The tea-house culture, temples, food and relaxed atmosphere make Chengdu one of China's most enjoyable cities even without the panda base.
